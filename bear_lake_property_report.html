📋 1  —  Executive Summary

Asking price: $49,900. This is a land-value-dominant deal wrapped around a distressed mobile home that will likely need to be removed. The listing's own language — "may need to be removed," "needs yard cleanup" — signals the mobile home is the liability, not the asset.

The land itself has genuine merit: residential-agricultural zoning, wooded rolling terrain, county road access, proximity to Bear Lake village, and a rural Michigan setting with electric service already at the road.

At $49,900 the price is roughly in line with comparable Pleasanton Hwy investment parcels (nearest comp: $59,900 for a similar distressed rural property nearby). However, buyers must budget seriously for what comes after closing.

All-in cost range: ~$54,000 (keep mobile home if livable)  to  ~$340,000 (tear down and build new stick-built home). The realistic middle path — tear down and replace with a new manufactured home — comes in at ~$151,000–$237,000.

This deal crosses from Risky into Good Value territory if a pre-purchase inspection confirms the mobile home is livable (or cheap to demo), the well and septic pass inspection, and no wetlands sit on the parcel.

⚠️ 4 — Major Risks
  • 🏚️
    Mobile home condition is completely unknown High Risk

    The listing says the mobile home "may need to be removed." Without an inspection you are buying blind. Pre-1976 mobile homes have no safety code compliance. Pre-1989 structures commonly contain asbestos. If the home is structurally unsound, flooded, or infested, full demolition is the only option.

  • ☣️
    Asbestos abatement could spike costs dramatically High Risk

    Michigan law requires an asbestos survey before demolishing any pre-1989 structure. Professional abatement on a single-wide mobile home runs $2,000–$8,000+. This cost is invisible until you open the walls.

  • 💧
    Well and septic status is unknown High Risk

    The listing mentions neither. If existing systems are functional → inspection cost ~$800–$1,500. If the septic has failed or needs replacement → $7,000–$35,000. If soil won't drain properly (common on rolling, wooded terrain), an engineered mound system is required: $15,000–$35,000+. If the well has failed → another $8,000–$15,000.

  • 🌿
    Possible wetlands on the parcel Medium-High Risk

    Pleasanton Township's zoning code has a Wetland Overlay District. Manistee County has extensive mapped wetlands. If any portion of this 0.87-acre parcel is in a regulated wetland, Michigan state permits are required before any digging, grading, or construction — and permits can be denied entirely. Check the state wetlands map before closing.

  • 📐
    Zoning may restrict replacement structure type Medium Risk

    Pleasanton Township has multiple RA sub-districts (RA-1, RA-2, RA-3) with different rules. A new manufactured/mobile home may need special approval depending on the sub-district. Tiny homes on wheels are almost certainly not permitted. Verify with the county planning department before purchasing.

  • 🌱
    Small lot limits homestead ambitions Medium Risk

    0.87 wooded acres on rolling hills leaves very limited flat, usable space after setbacks and tree cover. The practical gardening/building zone may be as small as 5,000–12,000 sq ft. Not suited for livestock or large food production.

  • 🔥
    Natural gas availability uncertain Low-Medium

    Superior Energy Company serves the Bear Lake village core, but whether a gas line actually runs to this rural address on Pleasanton Hwy is unconfirmed. Budget for propane delivery as the heating backup.

  • ❄️
    100–125 inches of annual snowfall Medium Risk

    Lake Michigan is 5 miles west, putting this area in a heavy lake-effect snow corridor. Foundation footings must go at least 42 inches deep by Michigan code. Heating costs, plowing, and winterization are real recurring expenses. An uninsulated mobile home will be very expensive to heat.

  • 🌊
    FEMA flood zone not yet confirmed Low-Medium

    "Rolling hills" description is encouraging, but the specific flood zone designation for this parcel has not been verified. If in a flood zone, insurance adds $800–$3,000/year to carrying costs.

  • 🚨
    Vacant distressed property attracts trouble Low-Medium

    If left empty during the planning/permitting phase, a visibly distressed mobile home invites vandalism, squatting, and theft (copper, appliances). Plan for either minimal security measures or expedited demolition.

🗺️ 10 — Recommended Strategy

Do these 6 things before committing to purchase. Most can be done with phone calls and free online tools in under a week.

  1. 1
    Order a mobile home inspection ($300–$500)

    Hire a Michigan licensed home inspector experienced with manufactured housing. This is non-negotiable — the entire value case rests on what they find.

  2. 2
    Check for wetlands on the parcel (free, 5 minutes)

    Go to: michigan.gov/egle/maps-data/wetlands-map-viewer and enter the address. If wetlands appear anywhere on the lot, get a professional wetland consultant before proceeding.

  3. 3
    Check the FEMA flood map (free)

    Go to: msc.fema.gov/portal/search — enter the address and confirm the property is in Zone X (minimal flood hazard).

  4. 4
    Call Manistee County Planning: (231) 929-5000

    Ask: "Can I place a new HUD-code manufactured home on a permanent foundation at 14638 Pleasanton Hwy after removing the existing mobile home?" Get verbal confirmation and ask for it in writing.

  5. 5
    Look up the property taxes online

    Go to: maps.liaa.org/manisteeparcels/propertysearch.asp and search for parcel number 26101-1202140002. This shows assessed value and annual tax amount.

  6. 6
    Request well & septic records from the seller

    Ask for any prior inspection records, well drilling logs, and septic permits. If they can't produce them, make a well water test and septic inspection a condition of purchase.

Two free online searches (wetlands + flood map) and two phone calls (county planning + inspect the home) can qualify or disqualify this deal in under a week. If everything comes back clean, move quickly — Pleasanton Hwy investment parcels at this price don't sit on the market indefinitely.
